What to bring on a sailing charter: the Lake Lanier packing list

Captain John Rice · 2026-06-12

By Captain John Rice, USCG-Licensed Master Captain (50-ton, Sail Endorsement) & Owner, Lord Nelson Charters -- operating on Lake Lanier since 2003 · 2026-06-05 · 9 min read

A couple from Cumming showed up at Dock Q a few summers ago in flip-flops, no sunscreen, and two bags of loose ice but nothing to keep their drinks cold. They had a good time on the water. But when they asked me afterward what they wished they had known, every answer came back to the same thing: nobody had told them what to bring on a sailing charter on Lake Lanier. This list fixes that.

Key takeaways

  • Layer up: Lake Lanier surface water temps average 80 to 84 degrees Fahrenheit in July and August but drop to the mid-60s in spring and fall, per Lake Lanier water monitoring data.
  • Georgia DNR requires children under 13 to wear a U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket at all times while the vessel is underway, per Georgia DNR boating regulations.
  • Lord Nelson Charters is fully BYOB with no corkage fee: beer, wine, and non-alcoholic drinks are all welcome aboard, per Captain John Rice booking policy.
  • SPF 50+ sunscreen applied 20 minutes before boarding and reapplied every 90 minutes is the minimum, per American Academy of Dermatology UV protection guidance.
  • Soft-soled, closed-toe shoes protect the deck and your ankles when the boat heels to 15 to 20 degrees off vertical.

What to bring on a sailing charter: clothing and footwear for Lake Lanier

For most guests, figuring out what to bring on a sailing charter starts with clothing -- and most people pack for the parking lot at Aqualand Marina rather than for two to seven hours on open water with reflected glare and a steady breeze over the main channel.

In July and August, when Lake Lanier surface temperatures reach 80 to 84 degrees Fahrenheit, light moisture-wicking shirts and shorts are fine. But bring a windbreaker or thin fleece regardless of the forecast. The channel between Buford Dam and Browns Bridge carries a consistent breeze, and 15 knots on a beam reach feels cooler than the parking lot suggested. Spring and fall sails are a different calculation altogether -- water temperatures drop into the mid-60s from October through April. A base layer plus a mid-layer plus a windshell gives you flexibility. You can take layers off. You cannot produce a jacket when you are two miles from Flowery Branch with a north wind building.

For footwear, closed-toe boat shoes or rubber-soled sneakers with a light-colored sole are the right choice. Fiberglass decks get slick when spray comes over the bow. Flip-flops are fine on the dock but change before boarding. Dark rubber soles mark gelcoat. Leather-soled dress shoes are a serious hazard on a heeling boat. Bring a wide-brim hat rated UPF 50 -- sailing hats go overboard more often than guests expect, so a backup in a dry bag is worth the space.

What to bring on a sailing charter: drinks, food, and the BYOB cooler

The drinks question is the first thing most guests ask when working out what to bring on a sailing charter. Lord Nelson Charters runs fully BYOB -- no corkage fee, no minimum purchase, no upcharge. Beer, wine, seltzers, lemonade, sparkling water: whatever your group wants is welcome aboard.

For the cooler, choose soft-sided over hard. A rigid cooler takes up cockpit space and becomes a tripping hazard when the boat heels. A 24-quart soft cooler holds two cases of canned drinks with room for ice. Pre-freeze drinks the night before and use block ice over cubed -- block ice lasts twice as long on a full-day sail out of Two Mile Creek. For food, stick to items that need no utensils and do not roll: grapes, cheese and crackers, cut fruit, protein bars, sandwiches halved. Avoid foil chip bags that catch wind and hot foods in containers that tip when the boat tacks. Bring a trash bag with tied handles -- keeping the cockpit clean is a shared responsibility. A 2.5-hour half-day goes by fast. A 7-hour full-day charter on Lake Lanier requires real meal planning.

Sun protection: what to bring on a sailing charter on open water

More guests underpack sun protection than any other category. The UV index on Lake Lanier runs 8 to 11 on clear summer days -- high enough that unprotected skin burns in under 15 minutes on open water between Buford Dam and Browns Bridge. After a full day on the main channel, a sunburn on the tops of your feet and the back of your neck is a medical problem, not a minor inconvenience. Here is what you actually need.

Start with SPF 50+ broad-spectrum sunscreen applied 20 minutes before stepping aboard at Aqualand Marina. Open water reflects UV radiation back up at you. American Academy of Dermatology UV protection guidance confirms that water surfaces increase total UV exposure by 10 to 25 percent compared to dry land. Reapply every 90 minutes -- set a phone alarm. You will not notice burning until it is already done.

A UPF-rated hat cuts facial UV exposure by more than 50 percent in direct sun, per American Academy of Dermatology sunscreen guidelines. Pair that with UV400 polarized sunglasses and a lightweight long-sleeve UPF 50+ shirt. UPF 50+ fabric blocks 98 percent of UV rays and covers the skin that sunscreen misses during perspiration. The UV index on Lake Lanier runs 8 to 11 on clear summer days, consistent with EPA UV Index scale guidance for high-radiation days. Index 8 means unprotected skin burns in under 15 minutes -- a number worth keeping in mind before you step on the boat.

For children, pack a kids' SPF 50+ spray, a rash guard, and a hat that stays on. Zinc-based formulas earn top marks in the EWG annual sunscreen guide for extended water wear.

What to leave at the car at Aqualand Marina before you board

A 37-foot sailboat heels 15 to 20 degrees on a beam reach and its cockpit holds far less usable storage than a typical car trunk. Anything that can roll will roll at the first powerboat wake; anything oversized blocks the main traffic path from the companionway to the bow. Leave everything that does not belong aboard at the parking lot at Aqualand Marina before you board.

Leave oversized Bluetooth speakers behind. The sound of the rigging and the wind on Lake Lanier is part of the experience. A small portable speaker at conversation volume is fine. A speaker loud enough to drown out the mainsail is not. Leave pets at home unless you have spoken with me beforehand -- it requires a specific conversation about the animal's temperament on a moving boat.

What to bring on a sailing charter that nobody tells first-timers

This is the part of what to bring on a sailing charter that standard packing lists almost always miss. Each item below comes from patterns I have seen across many seasons of guests asking me, after the fact, what they should have packed before boarding at Dock Q.

Motion sickness medication. Lake Lanier is generally calm, but wakes from powerboats and afternoon chop can affect guests who have not been on a sailboat before. Take it 30 minutes before boarding, not after symptoms start. Dramamine and Bonine are both available over-the-counter near I-985. If car trips make you queasy, bring it.

A dry bag for your phone and wallet. Not a sandwich bag -- an actual dry bag rated IPX6 or better. Phones drop at the dock during fender handling before the vessel has even left the slip. A 2-liter roll-top dry bag costs under 20 dollars and fits in a jacket pocket.

Cash or a payment app for gratuity. Tipping is entirely optional and never expected. Fifteen to twenty percent is the range guests typically use when they want to say thank you. I do not mention it during a charter and there is no tip jar. This note exists for guests who want to know and do not want to ask in the moment.

Hair ties if you have long hair. A beam reach at 12 to 15 knots puts sustained wind across the cockpit. Long hair in a sailing breeze is a visibility problem. Bring three -- one spare on your wrist.

A compressed layer even in July. A storm that passes the Lake Lanier area in late afternoon can drop air temperature 15 degrees in 20 minutes. A compressed down jacket the size of a softball changes a cold end-of-sail into a comfortable one. See the Lake Lanier sailing conditions guide by season for what to expect month by month.

A contact list in a waterproof sleeve. Phones die. A physical note with two contact numbers and a meeting point at the marina photo gallery is simple and practical insurance against a dead battery at the dock.

Life jackets and safety requirements for Lake Lanier charters

Part of knowing what to bring on a sailing charter is understanding what Captain John already carries for you. Georgia law requires at least one U.S. Coast Guard-approved wearable life jacket on board for every person on any recreational vessel on Lake Lanier. Georgia DNR further mandates that children under 13 must wear one at all times while the vessel is underway. Captain John carries properly sized life jackets for all guests.

The U.S. Coast Guard recreational boating safety standards define the Type I through Type V PFD classification system used on American waterways, including Lake Lanier. USCG-approved Type III inflatable PFDs offer good mobility for adults on inland waters like Lake Lanier without the bulk of older foam vests.

If you are sailing with young children for the first time, let them try on a properly fitted life jacket at home before the charter day. A child comfortable in a snug PFD on the couch will wear it for hours on the water. An oversized jacket is far less protective and harder for kids to tolerate. For our group and corporate sailing charters on Lake Lanier, Captain John carries enough life jackets for all passengers -- just notify me beforehand if you prefer to bring your own fitted inflatable PFD so the headcount is accurate at cast-off. Frequently asked questions

What sun protection should I bring on a Lake Lanier sailing charter?

When planning what to bring on a sailing charter for sun protection, start with SPF 50+ broad-spectrum sunscreen applied 20 minutes before boarding, polarized sunglasses with UV400 protection, and a wide-brim hat rated UPF 50 or higher. Lake Lanier open water reflects UV radiation, increasing total exposure. American Academy of Dermatology UV guidance confirms water reflection adds 10 to 25 percent to UV intensity at the surface. Reapply every 90 minutes. A lightweight long-sleeve UPF 50+ shirt is the most practical single addition after sunscreen and hat.

Does Lord Nelson Charters allow BYOB?

Yes, Lord Nelson Charters is a fully BYOB operation. You are welcome to bring your own beer, wine, seltzers, sodas, or any non-alcoholic drinks with no corkage fee and no upcharge. Soft-sided coolers pack into the cockpit better than rigid hard coolers. A 24-quart soft cooler holds enough drinks for four people on a half-day sail. Bring ice in a sealed bag. Glass bottles are permitted but discouraged on deck. Pre-chill drinks the night before to extend ice life on warm summer days at Aqualand Marina.

Do children need to wear life jackets on a Lake Lanier sailing charter?

Yes. Georgia law requires a U.S. Coast Guard-approved wearable personal flotation device for every person aboard, and Georgia DNR mandates that children under 13 must wear one at all times while underway on Lake Lanier. Captain John carries properly sized life jackets for child passengers. Let younger children try on a snug-fitting PFD before the day of the sail so they are comfortable wearing it. U.S. Coast Guard life jacket requirements define the Type I through Type III PFD classification used on U.S. recreational waterways.

What shoes are best for a sailing charter on Lake Lanier?

Non-slip, closed-toe boat shoes or rubber-soled sneakers with light-colored soles are the best footwear choice for anyone asking what to bring on a sailing charter in terms of footwear. Flip-flops slide on a wet fiberglass deck and provide no ankle support when the boat heels at 15 to 20 degrees. Bare feet are workable for experienced guests but risky near cleats and lines under load. Dark soles mark gelcoat. Boating shoes from Sperry or Merrell include deck-siping patterns for wet surfaces, typically priced between 60 and 120 dollars.
