BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier: what is allowed and what is not

Captain John Rice · 2026-09-06

By Captain John Rice, Captain & Owner, Lord Nelson Charters · 2026-07-22 · 9 min read

When couples call me to book, the first question is almost always the same: can we bring our own wine? On a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier trip out of Aqualand Marina, the short answer is yes. Georgia law allows adult passengers on a private vessel to drink, and I let guests bring what they like within a few rules. This post walks through the law, what I let aboard, why glass gets swapped for cans, and why I never drink while running the boat.

Key takeaways

  • Georgia law allows adult passengers on a private charter to drink; the Georgia DNR sets the operator BAC limit at 0.08%.
  • Every Lord Nelson Charters sail is bring-your-own out of Aqualand Marina, Dock Zk.
  • Cans and boxed wine are strongly preferred; broken glass on a teak deck is a real hazard flagged by U.S. Coast Guard boating safety guidance.
  • The captain, which is me, never drinks while the boat is underway, per U.S. Coast Guard recreational boating recommendations.
  • Bring water for every guest, since Lake Lanier heat plus alcohol is a top on-water risk highlighted by the Lake Lanier Association.

What a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier trip actually means

A BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail means you provide your own drinks and I provide the boat, the sail, and the captain. Lord Nelson Charters does not sell alcohol on board. That is a deliberate choice, and I never wanted to run a floating bar. A private charter is different from a commercial party boat: you are chartering the vessel for your group only, and Georgia treats what you drink on it as a private matter for adult passengers.

When you book, I ask you to bring drinks in a soft cooler if you have one. I keep a hard cooler on board and I supply ice for every sail. Most groups bring a mix: a bottle of prosecco for a toast, canned seltzer for the afternoon, water for the drive home. I do not police what you carry aboard so long as it fits the two rules below.

Practical scale matters. Lake Lanier is a 38,000-acre reservoir managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and the Corps publishes recreational use guidance that covers alcohol on federally managed waters. Georgia state law overlays that: passengers may drink; the operator may not. With me at the helm, that split is straightforward. Your group drinks. I do not.

Georgia open-container law and a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail

Georgia does not have an open-container ban for passengers on a private vessel. The rule that matters is boating under the influence. Under Georgia code, a boat operator over 0.08% blood alcohol is guilty of BUI. The Georgia Department of Natural Resources publishes the boating law with the current limits, penalties, and enforcement procedure. Read it before you book any charter on the lake, mine or anyone else's.

For passengers on a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier trip, you are not the operator. You can hold a drink, pour a drink, or drink a drink at any point during the sail. What you cannot do, and what I will step in to prevent, is offer one to me. I am running the boat. That is the whole point of hiring a captain.

Enforcement on the lake is real. The Georgia DNR Law Enforcement Division patrols the water actively on summer weekends, and the U.S. Coast Guard recreational boating safety office reports that alcohol is the leading known contributing factor in fatal recreational boating accidents nationally. That statistic drives every part of my alcohol policy, and it should shape yours before you cast off.

What I let guests bring on a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail

The short list of what I welcome aboard: beer and hard seltzer in cans; wine in cans, boxes, or plastic sleeves; premixed cocktails in cans; non-alcoholic drinks in any container. I ask that you skip full bottles of hard liquor and I ask that you skip glass wherever a canned alternative exists. Neither is a legal rule. Both are boat rules.

I cap the volume you carry aboard by common sense, not a written policy. A group of six for a four-hour sunset sail typically brings a 12-pack of light seltzer, a couple of bottles of wine, and a case of water. A bachelorette group of ten for a full-day sail might bring three times that. Whatever you bring, I want it in a cooler and I want water alongside it.

For sunset and evening sails, I remind guests that Lake Lanier gets cool fast once the sun drops behind the trees at Browns Bridge. Bring one extra layer per guest. Glass vs cans: what works on the water

Cans win on a sailboat. Glass looks nice on a table on land, but on a moving deck it becomes a liability the moment a wake hits or a guest sets it down on a curved surface. Over the years I have watched wine bottles roll off cockpit seats more than once. None broke, but each was close.

The physics matter. A sailboat heels, which is a fancy word for tips, under sail. My boat routinely heels ten to fifteen degrees on a normal Lake Lanier afternoon breeze. A can with a wide base stays put. A bottle with a narrow bottom does not. When glass breaks on a teak or fiberglass deck, the shards get into non-obvious places, and the U.S. Coast Guard boating safety guidance explicitly warns about cut hazards from broken glass on decks.

For groups who insist on a bottle of champagne for a toast on a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail (engagement sails, anniversary sails, rehearsal dinner sails), I keep a small cushioned bottle holder in the cockpit. Open the bottle at anchor, pour into plastic flutes, and stow the empty. That is the rule. Booking a private engagement or proposal sail? Text me before the trip and I will have the bottle holder out.

Why the captain does not drink

I do not drink on the boat. Ever. Not one beer at the dock, not one glass of wine at anchor, not a sip of your champagne at your toast. This is not because I am anti-alcohol. It is because I am solely responsible for the boat, the sail plan, and every guest aboard during a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail.

The U.S. Coast Guard boating safety office publishes a clear operator standard: do not drink and drive a boat. Georgia state law adds a legal BAC ceiling of 0.08% for operators, per the Georgia DNR. I stay at 0.00%. That gives me full margin for the routine things (reading a squall line on the horizon, docking in wind, adjusting sail trim in a gust) and full margin for the unusual things too.

The Lake Lanier Association documents the local pattern: summer weekend traffic on Lake Lanier is heavy, wake from powerboats is constant, and the mix of alcohol and open water is the most common source of avoidable incidents. My job is to keep that risk on your side of the boat, not mine.

Frequently asked questions

Can passengers drink on a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier trip?

Yes. Georgia does not restrict passengers on a private vessel from consuming alcohol. Adult guests on a Lord Nelson Charters sail can bring their own beer, wine, seltzer, or premixed drinks and drink them during the trip. The captain, which is me, does not drink and never has while running the boat. The Georgia Department of Natural Resources publishes the current boating alcohol law, which sets the operator BAC limit at 0.08% and leaves passenger consumption unregulated. Read it once before you book so you understand the split between operator and passenger rules on Lake Lanier.

Do you sell drinks on board?

No. Every Lord Nelson Charters sail is bring-your-own, which means you supply what you want to drink and I supply the boat, the cooler, and the ice. I do not hold a liquor license and I have deliberately kept the operation single-captain and private-charter only. That model works because it keeps the boat quiet, keeps groups small, and keeps the alcohol decision entirely with the guests. The U.S. Coast Guard recreational boating safety materials actually encourage this split: passenger choice, operator sobriety.

Is glass allowed on a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail?

Not banned, but strongly discouraged. Cans and boxed wine are safer on a moving, heeling deck. If you must bring a bottle for an engagement toast, anniversary champagne, or birthday sparkling, tell me ahead of time and I will rig the cushioned bottle holder in the cockpit. Once the bottle is empty, I stow it immediately and it does not come back out. Broken glass on a teak deck is a real injury risk flagged by the U.S. Coast Guard boating safety office. One toast, one bottle, then cans the rest of the sail.

What happens if a passenger gets too drunk?

I turn the boat back. Every Lord Nelson Charters sail is captained by me personally, and if a guest becomes a safety risk to themselves or others aboard, I return to Aqualand Marina and end the charter early. In more than twenty years running charters on Lake Lanier, this has happened rarely, and both times the group agreed with the call. I do not refund the trip when this happens. The guest signed on knowing the rule. The Lake Lanier Association safety pages reinforce the same standard for private captains on the lake.

Can we bring hard liquor on a BYOB boat charter Lake Lanier sail?

I ask groups to keep it to canned premixed cocktails rather than a full bottle of spirits. This is not a legal rule; it is a practical one. Bottles of tequila or whiskey on a moving deck become pour-mistakes, spills, and, in the sun, much higher BAC per guest than the group planned for. Canned premixed cocktails from the ready-to-drink shelf give you the same drink experience with a controlled pour. The U.S. Coast Guard operator safety guidance is clear that alcohol intake escalates faster on water than on land.

Do you provide cups, ice, and a cooler?

Yes. I keep a hard cooler on board every sail, I supply ice, and I keep a stack of insulated plastic cups in the galley. You bring the drinks; I handle the rest. If your group is larger, such as a full boat of fourteen for a birthday charter, I ask that you also bring a soft cooler of your own so we have room for everyone's drinks and enough space for water.
