Lake Lanier sailing etiquette: guest rules for a private charter

Captain John Rice · 2026-10-16

By Captain John Rice, Captain & Owner, Lord Nelson Charters · 2026-10-16 · 12 min read

Flowery Branch on a June Saturday: wake boats everywhere, a light southerly, and six guests standing on my foredeck wondering where to put their bags. That moment is what Lake Lanier sailing etiquette is really about. It is not formality or yacht club manners. It is a short set of habits covering where you sit, what you carry aboard, and when you stop talking and listen, and those habits are what keep a private charter safe and relaxed.

Key takeaways

  • Guests follow the skipper's instructions during sailing maneuvers, which is how crew behavior is taught by US Sailing.
  • Keeping a proper lookout is everyone's job on deck, not only the captain's, per USCGBoating.org.
  • Basic right-of-way rules are treated as part of safe sailing practice by American Sailing.
  • Georgia requires a wearable approved life jacket for every person aboard and requires children under 13 to wear one underway, per Georgia DNR.
  • Lake Lanier covers roughly 38,000 acres with about 692 miles of shoreline, per the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.
  • Charters get delayed or cut short when storms build over north Georgia, based on forecasts from the National Weather Service.

What guests should know before stepping aboard: Lake Lanier sailing etiquette starts at the dock

Three things matter before you step aboard at Aqualand Marina, Dock Zk: pack soft bags instead of hard coolers, wear flat soled shoes that will not leave black marks, and listen all the way through the safety briefing. I run more than 150 charters a season out of this dock, and the guests who skip one of those three are the ones who end up uncomfortable by the second hour. Everything after that is comfort, and those three keep the deck safe and the departure on time.

The briefing covers where the life jackets live, how to move around the cockpit, and what we do if someone goes over the side. The U.S. Coast Guard recreational boating safety program treats a proper lookout and safe practice underway as continuous habits rather than a one time announcement, and that is how I run it. If you have sailed before, say so. If you never have, say that too, and skim our first-timer's guide to a Lake Lanier sailing charter before you drive up.

Georgia law sits underneath all of it. Georgia DNR boating regulations require a wearable, approved life jacket for every person on board, and children under 13 have to wear one while the boat is underway. Most of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette works like that: half courtesy, half state law, and the guests who read a little before they arrive have a better day.

Scale is part of the picture. Lake Lanier holds roughly 38,000 acres of water with about 692 miles of shoreline, managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Mobile District. On a summer weekend a lot of that water is busy, and a big crowded lake is why these habits exist at all.

Moving, sitting, and stowing gear: Lake Lanier sailing etiquette once we clear Dock Zk

Move with one hand for the boat and one for yourself, sit where I point you, and send bags below into the cabin instead of leaving them loose in the cockpit. A sailboat heels, meaning it leans over as the wind fills the sails, sometimes to fifteen or twenty degrees on a gusty day. That lean is normal, it is not a sign of trouble, and it is far easier to ride out sitting down than standing.

The boom swings across the cockpit during a tack, when the bow turns through the wind, or a gybe, when the stern turns through the wind, so I call it out before either happens and guests duck. Stay off the foredeck while we are underway unless I invite you forward. Keep fingers clear of winches and out of any line that is under load. Hats and sunglasses want a strap, because the lake keeps everything that goes over the side. Our dress guide for Lake Lanier charters covers shoes and layers in more detail.

Loose gear is the quiet hazard, and I learned that the hard way. Two summers ago a guest's glass water bottle got loose in the cockpit during a tack, rolled under my own foot at the exact moment I needed to step to the new side, and put me on the deck hard enough to bruise a hip and nearly lose my grip on the mainsheet. Nobody else was hurt and the boat was fine, but it is the closest call I have had on this boat, and it is why stowing gear is not a suggestion anymore. Good Lake Lanier sailing etiquette means stowing whatever you are not holding, which is also why soft bags beat hard sided coolers on a boat with a narrow companionway, the stepped opening that leads below deck.

Lake Lanier sailing etiquette that helps the captain run the boat safely

Two guest habits help me more than anything else: quiet during maneuvers, and extra eyes on the water. On a busy July Saturday I can count two dozen other boats inside a single cove, and an extra set of eyes catches what I cannot see past the sail. US Sailing crew education teaches that crew members follow the skipper's instructions during sailing activities, and that is exactly the arrangement on a private charter.

When I say ready about, conversation pauses, heads come down, and nobody stands up until the sails are set on the new side. It takes about ten seconds. American Sailing's sailing fundamentals curriculum puts basic right-of-way and lookout at the core of safe practice, and guests do not need to memorize the rules to help with the lookout half of that. If you see a boat, a swimmer, a paddleboard, or a floating log, say so out loud. I would rather hear the same pontoon called out four times than miss it once.

Traffic near Browns Bridge and in the mouth of Two Mile Creek gets heavy on weekends, and a sailboat under sail is slow to change course. Conditions matter too. I check NOAA marine and weather information before every charter, and part of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette is accepting that the captain's call on weather is final. Our guide to when captains delay a charter for thunderstorms shows how that decision gets made.

Horn signals are the other piece worth knowing, mostly so they do not startle you. Short blasts are how boats talk to each other on the water, and part of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette is understanding that five or more short blasts is not rudeness. It means danger or doubt.

Food, drinks, music, and photos: Lake Lanier sailing etiquette for an easy deck

Bring what you like within reason: no glass, nothing that needs a stove, and trash goes home with us. On a typical four-hour half-day sail, guests go through about one soft cooler of drinks and snacks, which is roughly what fits below without crowding the companionway. Music is welcome at conversation volume. Photos are welcome from anywhere except standing on the bow while we are sailing, which is the one spot I will ask you to leave.

Alcohol is fine for adult guests, and I am not drinking, since someone needs full attention on the wind, the traffic, and the next weather change. Keep cans and bottles in a soft cooler and keep the trash aboard until we are back at the dock. The Lake Lanier Association spends a large number of volunteer hours pulling litter off this shoreline, and leaving nothing behind is the simplest courtesy on the lake.

Music carries much farther over water than people expect. In a quiet cove at sunset, a speaker at full volume reaches every other boat anchored there. Explore Georgia visitor information lists Lake Lanier among the state's busiest outdoor destinations, so assume you have neighbors within earshot. Lake Lanier sailing etiquette on music is simple: if you cannot hold a normal conversation over it, turn it down.

Drones are a no from me. Photos from the cockpit and the cabin top are easy, and if you want a shot of the boat under full sail, ask and I will hold a steady leg for it. See our guide to proposing on a sailboat if the photograph is the whole point of the day.

Small customs that round out Lake Lanier sailing etiquette on a private charter

The customs that matter on a private charter are small ones: ask before you touch a line, help with dock lines only when asked, keep the head door latched, and let the captain finish docking before anyone stands up, a process that takes about two minutes at Dock Zk even with a crosswind. None of it is formal. All of it saves time and scuffed gelcoat.

Boats passing each other on Lake Lanier still wave. Sailors give each other swinging room, the open circle a boat needs to pivot around its anchor without hitting a neighbor, in a cove rather than anchoring on top of a boat that got there first. If a boat looks like it is in trouble, we go look, and that is not optional on any lake. The part of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette that first-timers find surprising is how much of it is simply paying attention to other people's day.

Guests often ask whether they can steer. Usually yes, on a steady leg, and I will say when the moment is not right. Helping with the sails is welcome too. Ask. Frequently asked questions

What should I wear on a sailing charter on Lake Lanier?

Flat soled, non-marking shoes, a layer you can take off, a hat with a strap, and sunglasses. Skip heels, skip black soles, and skip anything you would be upset to get wet. Mornings in spring and fall on the lake run cooler than they do in town, and the National Weather Service forecast office in Peachtree City is the forecast I watch for north Georgia conditions. Bring sunscreen as a stick or lotion rather than a spray can, because spray drifts onto the deck and makes it slippery. Closed-toe boat shoes with white or non-marking soles work best, and flip-flops are the one thing that causes more slips than anything else on a wet deck. Most guests overdress for heat and underdress for wind.

Can guests drink alcohol on a private sailing charter?

Adult guests can drink on board, and I do not, because somebody needs a clear head to read the wind and the traffic. Georgia treats boating under the influence seriously, and Georgia DNR publishes the state boating regulations that cover operators and passengers alike. Practically, cans beat bottles, a soft cooler beats a hard one, and water in between keeps people steady on a moving, heeling deck. Sun, wind, and motion make drinks land harder than they do on shore, which catches first-timers out more than anything else. All trash, cans included, stays aboard until we are back at the dock at Aqualand Marina. If anyone gets unsteady on their feet or in their judgment, we shorten the sail and head in early.

Do children have to wear life jackets the whole time on the boat?

Children under 13 must wear an approved wearable life jacket whenever the boat is underway in Georgia, and every person aboard needs one available regardless of age. The U.S. Coast Guard recreational boating safety program recommends wearing a jacket rather than stowing it, which is what I ask of any guest who is not a confident swimmer. I carry properly sized jackets, and sizing matters more than people expect, because an adult jacket on a small child does not hold them the right way up. Life jacket rules are the one part of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette that is not negotiable.

What happens if a thunderstorm builds while we are out on the lake?

We head in early, and that call is mine, not a vote among guests who want to finish the sail. North Georgia builds afternoon storms quickly in summer, often within an hour, and National Weather Service lightning safety guidance is blunt about it: there is no safe place outside during a thunderstorm, and a sailboat with a tall aluminum mast is not where you want to be. I watch the radar before departure and again through the sail, and I would rather cut a trip short than gamble on a storm cell that is still an hour out. Sometimes that means a shorter trip, sometimes a delayed start, and sometimes a different date altogether. Guests who treat the weather call as part of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette rather than a disappointment get a better day on the water, because the alternative is sitting out a storm on open water with nowhere to go.

How early should we arrive at Aqualand Marina before the sail?

Plan to be at the dock about fifteen minutes before the scheduled start. That covers parking, the walk out to Dock Zk, the safety briefing, and loading bags without anyone rushing. Aqualand sits near Flowery Branch just off I-985, and the City of Flowery Branch publishes local visitor information if you are making a day of it. Traffic leaving Atlanta on a Friday afternoon is the usual reason guests run late, so build in a buffer. Parking at Aqualand fills fast on weekend mornings, so leave extra time if you are arriving after nine, and text ahead if you think you will run more than a few minutes behind. A late arrival shortens the sail rather than pushing it back, because the wind and the light keep their own schedule.

How do sailboats share coves and anchorages on a busy weekend?

The first boat into a cove sets the pattern, and everyone after that gives it swinging room rather than anchoring on top of it, usually at least fifty feet when the cove allows it. Lake Lanier draws heavy weekend traffic from the Atlanta metro area, and with 692 miles of shoreline, popular coves still fill by midday because most boats cluster in the same half dozen spots. Under sail we stay clear of swim areas and anchored boats, and we keep wake in mind even though a sailboat under sail makes very little of it. The part of Lake Lanier sailing etiquette that applies here is patience: if a cove is full, there is almost always another one a mile away, and circling waiting for a spot to open is worse manners than just moving on.
