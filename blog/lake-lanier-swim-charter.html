Lake Lanier swim charter: plan a safe day for water-loving guests

Captain John Rice · 2026-11-06

By Captain John Rice, Captain & Owner, Lord Nelson Charters · USCG Master 50-ton license · 11 years on Lake Lanier · 2026-11-06 · 10 min read

"Can we swim, or do we just look at the water?" That question comes up before almost anything else when a group plans a Lake Lanier swim charter out of Aqualand Marina. The short answer is yes, swimming works well on a private sail, but only when the cove, the hour, and the forecast line up. Heat, sun, and afternoon storms decide more about your day than the boat does.

Key takeaways

  • Swimming happens at anchor with the engine off and the ladder down, which keeps a swim stop inside the on-deck habits taught by American Sailing.
  • Water reflects ultraviolet radiation and increases exposure, so check the forecast UV Index before you pack, per NOAA.
  • The National Weather Service Atlanta forecast office issues heat products using heat-index thresholds, with a Heat Advisory for qualifying values of at least 105.
  • Thunder ends a swim stop on the spot, because boating during a thunderstorm is unsafe according to NOAA.
  • Half-day and full-day sails usually fit 1 or 2 swim stops, while a sunset sail fits at most 1.

Can guests swim during a Lake Lanier swim charter?

Yes. A swim stop is a planned twenty-to-forty-minute anchor break built into the sail, not a separate swim trip, and it runs the same way on nearly every warm-weather Lake Lanier swim charter: the boat anchors in a sheltered cove, the engine shuts off, the ladder goes down, and nobody enters the water until I call the stern clear.

The lake does not behave like a pool. It is a reservoir held behind Buford Dam, and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir management program runs the releases that move surface level and current through the season. Swimmers feel that as a cool layer a few feet down and a shoreline sitting higher or lower than it did on their last visit. None of it is dangerous on its own. It just means the cove I used in May can be the wrong cove in September.

Life jackets get settled at the dock, before we leave Dock Zk. Everyone aboard has a fitted jacket, weak swimmers wear one in the water, and children wear what Georgia law requires. The Coast Guard recreational boating safety program publishes the fit and wear guidance I work from, and the Georgia Department of Natural Resources boating program is where the state rules live. For the longer version of that side of the day, read our guide to what Georgia law requires before you sail.

There is a full breakdown of this topic in Lake Lanier fishing and sailing: rules for a mixed-group charter day.

How to pick the best swim stop and timing for a Lake Lanier swim charter

Pick the stop by wind direction and boat traffic, then pick the hour by sun and heat. A cove on the lee shore stays flat while open water chops up, and mid-morning or the last two hours before sunset put far less sun on bare shoulders than a one o'clock swim does.

Sun is the part groups underestimate. NOAA sun safety and UV Index guidance advises checking the UV Index because water can reflect ultraviolet radiation and increase exposure, so a swimmer floating on their back is taking sun from above and off the surface at the same time. I read the index the night before along with wind and cloud cover, and it changes both which cove I choose and how long we stay in.

Location matters as much as the hour. The coves near Two Mile Creek and the shoreline south toward Flowery Branch give shelter from a north wind, and when the wind swings I go the other way instead of arguing with it. Our piece on islands, parks, and scenic shores covers the water we cross to reach them, and the Flowery Branch parks and recreation pages help if you want a shoreside backup plan. A July sail looks nothing like one in late September, mostly because of where the wind sits.

What swimmers should bring on a Lake Lanier swim charter

Bring more sun protection and more drinking water than you think you need, plus a dry change of clothes: most swimmers pack one towel and need two. A Lake Lanier swim charter runs on a boat, not in a bathhouse, so water shoes and a bag that closes do more for comfort than anything else on the list.

  • A rash guard or UPF shirt for every swimmer, worn in the water rather than packed.
  • Broad-spectrum sunscreen, reapplied after each swim and not just once in the parking lot.
  • Water shoes, because ladders and decks get slick and shorelines are rocky.
  • Two towels per swimmer: one for the water, one that stays dry for the ride back.
  • A hat and sunglasses with a strap, since both go overboard easily under sail.
  • Drinking water for the whole group, plus a soft-sided cooler that stows flat.
  • A dry bag or zip bag for phones and keys.

Leave the glass at home. Our charter checklist for safety gear and comfort has the full packing list, and American Sailing's education library is a good place to read about moving around a boat safely before a first sail.

How captains manage boat traffic and water conditions on a Lake Lanier swim charter

On a Lake Lanier swim charter I manage boat traffic by choosing water other boats skip, since weekend wake on the main channel can run a foot or more and makes swimming rough. The stop goes into a cove off the traffic line, anchored bow to the wind, with the ladder set on the sheltered side.

Heat is the next call, and I learned that one the hard way. Two Augusts ago I let a swim stop run a full forty minutes in a Two Mile Creek cove on a day the heat index had already crossed 105, and two guests came up the ladder dizzy and spent the rest of the sail resting in the cabin. I cap stops at fifteen to twenty minutes now once that threshold hits, no matter how much the group wants to stay in. The National Weather Service Atlanta forecast office issues heat products using heat-index thresholds, including a Heat Advisory for qualifying values of at least 105 degrees Fahrenheit, and that is the number I watch. On days like that we leave earlier, guests sit in the shade of the sails between swims, and the stop gets shorter. Our guide to reading wind, heat, and rain in the forecast walks through what I check the night before. A 105 degree heat-index day is a morning trip or it is a different day.

Storms end the discussion. NOAA boating safety guidance states that boating during a thunderstorm is unsafe because hearing thunder means lightning may be close enough to strike, and the National Weather Service lightning safety pages say it in plainer words: when thunder roars, go indoors. In practice, swimmers are out of the water and the boat is pointed home before the first cell gets close. We cover the timing of that decision in our post on when captains delay a charter.

What a Lake Lanier swim charter day looks like hour by hour

A half-day sail with a swim stop usually runs about four hours: gear and brief at the dock, sail out, anchor and swim, then sail back. The timing is built around sun and wind, which is why the same four hours feel completely different starting at nine in the morning and starting at two in the afternoon.

Most groups book a Lake Lanier swim charter as a half day and are surprised how much of it is actual sailing. That is the point. The swim is a stop inside a sail, and the sail is what makes the stop feel like something other than a crowded public beach.

Questions to ask before you book a Lake Lanier swim charter

Ask who makes the decisions and how the day gets called off. On a single-captain boat where I have run every sail myself for eleven years, those answers are short: I pick the swim stop, I make the weather call, and I am the one driving. Ask these before you put money on a date.

  • Is there a boarding ladder, and how do swimmers get back aboard without help?
  • Who stays aboard and on watch while guests are in the water?
  • Are life jackets aboard in the sizes my group needs, including children?
  • What happens to the plan on a heat advisory day or a storm afternoon?
  • Where do we leave from, and how long is the drive down I-985?

If your group is new to boats, US Sailing's safety education program is worth a read beforehand, and Explore Georgia's Lake Lanier visitor pages help with the rest of the weekend. For current conditions and shoreline stewardship work, the Lake Lanier Association's water quality monitoring program publishes updates through the warm season.

Frequently asked questions

Can you swim off a sailboat on Lake Lanier?

Yes, and it is one of the most common requests I get in July and August. On a Lake Lanier swim charter the boat anchors in a sheltered cove, the engine is shut down, and a stern ladder goes in so swimmers can get back aboard without help. Someone stays on deck the whole time, and anyone who is not a confident swimmer wears a fitted jacket in the water. The Coast Guard recreational boating safety program publishes the life jacket fit and wear guidance I use at the dock.

When is the water warm enough for a swim stop?

Surface water warms through late spring and holds its heat into early fall, so most swim stops happen between late May and late September. Below the first few feet it stays noticeably cooler, which is why swimmers treading water feel a cold layer on their legs. Lake level and current also shift with releases run by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir management program, so a cove that was waist deep at the shoreline in June can look different by September. I check conditions before every trip rather than going by the calendar.

Do kids need life jackets during the swim stop?

Children wear life jackets in the water on my boat, and Georgia law sets requirements for young children aboard regardless of what the group prefers. The Georgia Department of Natural Resources boating program publishes the current age and wear rules, and I carry child sizes so nobody is stuck in an adult jacket that rides up over their ears. Tell me ages and weights when you book so the right sizes are aboard. Parents should also plan one adult in the water per young swimmer, which matters more than any piece of gear.

What happens if a thunderstorm shows up while we are swimming?

Swimmers come out and we head in. NOAA states that boating during a thunderstorm is unsafe because hearing thunder means lightning may be close enough to strike, and National Weather Service lightning safety guidance treats audible thunder as the signal to stop outdoor activity. I watch radar before we leave the dock and again from the water, because summer afternoons on this lake build cells fast. If the call comes early enough, we move the swim stop forward or reschedule the sail instead of gambling on a gap between cells.

How long is the swim stop on a half-day sail?

Plan on twenty to forty minutes in the water on a four hour sail, with the rest of the time spent sailing. Heat changes that. The National Weather Service Atlanta forecast office issues heat products using heat-index thresholds, including a Heat Advisory for qualifying values of at least 105 degrees Fahrenheit, and on those days I shorten the stop and keep guests under the shade of the sails between dips. A full-day Lake Lanier swim charter can hold two shorter stops in different coves, which usually beats one long one for groups with kids.

What should I bring for a swim stop on a sailing charter?

Two towels per swimmer, water shoes, a rash guard or UPF shirt, broad-spectrum sunscreen, a hat with a strap, and drinking water. NOAA sun safety guidance on the UV Index advises checking it because water can reflect ultraviolet radiation and increase exposure, which is why I push sun cover harder than most guests expect for a Lake Lanier swim charter. Pack everything in a soft bag that closes, leave glass at home, and bring a dry change of clothes for the ride back up I-985. Phones go in a zip bag or stay in the cabin.
