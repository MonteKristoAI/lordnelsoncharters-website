Lake Lanier Sailing Charter for Families: What to Know Before Booking

Captain John Rice · 2026-05-03

Key takeaways

  • Georgia DNR requires all children under 13 to wear a US Coast Guard-approved life jacket while a vessel is underway; Lord Nelson Charters carries sizes for every passenger on board.
  • Ages 8 to 14 get the most from a Lake Lanier sailing trip; younger kids enjoy it but do best on calm days with expectations set beforehand.
  • Lake Lanier's 38,000 acres gives families room to find sheltered water near Two Mile Creek or open runs past Browns Bridge, depending on wind and traffic that day.
  • Captain John Rice personally captains every charter: no hired crew, no strangers at the helm.
  • Motion sickness is manageable; staying on deck and watching the horizon outperforms any tablet taken after symptoms start, per the National Library of Medicine.

If you are searching for a Lake Lanier sailing charter for your family, the first thing to know is that a sailboat is not a party boat with a playlist. What you get is wind, water, and something rare on a weekend outing with kids: a stretch of time where they actually pay attention. I have been running Lord Nelson Charters out of Aqualand Marina on Lake Lanier since around 2003, and family day sails are some of the trips I remember longest. This guide covers what 20-plus years on the water have taught me about making a family charter work.

Why a sailboat day is different from a pontoon party

A pontoon rental and a sailing charter both get you on the water, but the experience is almost nothing alike. A pontoon is loud, usually crowded, and revolves around the cooler. A sailing charter is quieter, more purposeful, and gives your family something most weekend outings do not: extended time without screens and with things that are actually worth watching.

Once we clear the no-wake zone past Dock Q and the sail goes up, the engine cuts off. You hear water against the hull. Kids who were staring at phones put them down. The boat heels slightly as wind fills the sail, which surprises first-timers and delights most kids. On a hot August afternoon, the breeze Lake Lanier generates on open water is the best air you will find within an hour of Atlanta.

Sailing moves at a pace that rewards looking around. Browns Bridge comes into view slowly. You spot herons working the shallows near the Flowery Branch shore. You notice channel markers, the tree line, other boats. That unhurried pace is the point, and children respond to it in ways that often surprise their parents.

If speed or a swim stop is the priority, a motorboat rental may suit you better. If you want your kids asking "what does that line do?" this is the trip.

The age question: what works at 5, at 12, and at 16

Younger children can absolutely come on a family sailing trip, but what they take from the experience varies a great deal by age. The 8-to-12 window is the sweet spot, though I have had good trips with 5-year-olds and engaged 16-year-olds alike. Here is an honest breakdown from thousands of hours on Lake Lanier.

Age rangeWhat they typically experienceTips for parents
5-7 High curiosity, short attention span. Loves the heel and the sound of wind in the sail. May be restless after two hours. Bring snacks and a small activity. Plan to sit with them at the bow for stretches of the trip. Manage your expectations for the second half.
8-12 The golden window. Old enough to follow a basic sailing explanation, strong enough to pull a line, curious enough to stay engaged for four full hours. Let them help where safe. Coiling a dock line at the end of the trip is a good starting point. I involve kids at my own discretion.
13-16 Can hold the tiller briefly under close supervision. Understands wind angles. Good at spotting markers and other vessels. Frame this as learning a real skill, not just a boat ride. Teenagers respond to that framing. Mention their interest when you book.

Children under 5 need an adult focused on them throughout the trip. The boat has lines, cleats, and companionway edges that are not child-proofed. That said, I have taken 4-year-olds who stayed absorbed from departure to dock. If you are unsure about your child, message me before booking and I will give you a straight answer.

Safety on board: life jackets and the captain at the helm

Safety on a small sailboat is simple but worth reviewing before you arrive at Aqualand Marina. Georgia DNR requires children under 13 to wear a personal flotation device while underway on Lake Lanier, regardless of boat type or size. Lord Nelson Charters carries approved life jackets for every passenger, and every child wears one while we are moving.

A note on how the trip runs: I stay at the helm. No autopilot, no crew. When I am trimming sail or watching weather build to the northwest, I need your children seated or in a designated area. I point that area out before we leave the dock. It is a simple ask and it keeps everyone safe.

The US Coast Guard recreational boating safety guidelines are clear that wearing a life jacket outright, rather than simply having one on board, is the single largest factor in surviving an unexpected fall overboard. We follow that without exception.

On swimming: I do not stop for swim breaks on Lake Lanier. The lake has submerged structures in some areas, and the Georgia Environmental Protection Division issues periodic blue-green algae advisories that make open-water swimming a genuine health risk. If your children expect a swim stop, clarify that before booking.

What to pack for a 4-hour family charter

Keep it light. A 30-foot sailboat is not a ferry, and loose gear in the cockpit creates tripping hazards for every passenger on board. Here is what the families who have the best days actually bring on a Lake Lanier sailing charter, and what they wisely leave at the dock.

  • Sunscreen, SPF 50 or higher. There is no shade on the water after 10am, and the reflection off Lake Lanier doubles UV exposure. The Skin Cancer Foundation's sun protection guidance recommends reapplying every two hours on open water. Bring more than you think you need.
  • Water and snacks. One litre per person minimum on a summer day. Granola bars, crackers, and whole fruit travel well. Avoid anything messy or strongly scented. The boat has cooler space but bring your own drinks.
  • A light layer. Lake Lanier runs cooler than Atlanta in the morning and the on-water breeze surprises people even at 75°F. One long-sleeve shirt or thin fleece per person is enough.
  • Non-slip footwear or bare feet. No sandals with straps that catch on deck hardware. Deck shoes, athletic shoes, or bare feet all work fine.
  • Motion sickness medication if relevant. Take it one hour before boarding, not after symptoms start. More on this below.
  • A small dry bag for phones and valuables. Water spray is part of sailing. Anything left on deck may get wet.

Leave large bags and inflatable pool toys at home. For a complete packing checklist, see my post on what to pack for a Lake Lanier day charter.

Weather, bathroom, motion sickness: the three things every parent asks

Three questions come up in almost every booking email I get from families with children: what happens if the weather turns bad on the water, where does everyone use the bathroom, and what if someone gets motion sickness on the boat. Here are straight answers to each.

Weather

Lake Lanier weather is not gentle in summer. Afternoon thunderstorms build fast from the southwest, and I have cancelled or cut trips short because of them. I check the National Weather Service Atlanta forecast every morning and make a go/no-go call by 8am. If I cancel due to weather, you get a full reschedule at no penalty. Morning departures from Aqualand Marina tend to run calmer. If your family needs schedule predictability, ask about an early start when you book.

Bathroom

The boat has a marine head: small, functional, and fine for a four-hour trip. I ask that everyone handle anything serious before boarding; Aqualand Marina has dockside restrooms. Any child who has managed a train or plane bathroom will have no trouble.

Motion sickness

Lake Lanier is not open ocean. The chop is usually short and irregular rather than long rolling swell. Even so, some people get sick on any moving boat. If your child gets car sick, plan for it. Dramamine for Kids or Bonine taken at least one hour before boarding is far more effective than taking it after symptoms start. On the water, staying on deck, watching the horizon, and avoiding screens all help. The National Library of Medicine's motion sickness page covers non-medication options clearly. If someone does get sick, we slow the boat and handle it without fuss.

Frequently asked questions

Here are the questions I hear most often from Atlanta-area parents planning a family sailing trip on Lake Lanier. If something is not covered, reach out and I will answer before you pick a date.

What is the minimum age for a Lake Lanier sailing charter?

No hard cutoff, but I ask parents to be realistic. Children under 3 are a poor fit: the boat is not baby-proofed, and fitting an infant life jacket correctly requires specialised training I do not hold. For ages 4 and up, I have had many successful family trips. If your child is under 5, message me before booking so we can talk through conditions expected that day. Children 8 and older almost always have a great time. If your child has anxiety about water, mention it ahead of booking so we can discuss it.

Do we need to bring our own life jackets for the kids?

No. Lord Nelson Charters carries US Coast Guard-approved personal flotation devices in children's and adult sizes, and you do not need to bring your own. You are welcome to bring a properly rated jacket if your child has a strong fit preference. All children under 13 will wear a life jacket while the boat is underway on Lake Lanier. That is both Georgia DNR law and my own standard operating procedure regardless of conditions or how close to shore we are.

What happens if the weather turns bad mid-trip?

I watch radar the entire time we are on the water, especially during the June-to-September storm season. When a cell builds toward us, I drop sail and return to Aqualand Marina under power before it arrives. If we cut a trip short due to weather, I pro-rate the charter time and credit the difference toward a future booking. Trips cancelled before departure due to a storm warning are fully rescheduled at no additional cost. I have not had a family caught in a storm on Lake Lanier and intend to keep that record.

Can my teenager take the wheel?

Yes, briefly and under close supervision. When conditions are calm and we have clear water away from the Buford Dam zone and main channel traffic, I will let a teenager hold the tiller and feel how wind and sail interact. It is a real skill with real consequences, which is what makes it stick. I make the call based on conditions and how the trip is going. If your teenager is interested, mention it when you book and I will keep it in mind throughout the charter.

Is Lake Lanier a good lake for sailing?

It is one of the better inland sailing lakes in the Southeast. At 38,000 acres, there is space to find wind without constant motorboat wake. The prevailing southwest breeze in summer gives a reliable afternoon fill near Two Mile Creek and off the Flowery Branch shore. For water levels, conditions, and seasonal advisories before your trip, the Lake Lanier Association publishes regular updates worth reading before your first time out.
