Wednesday group sail Lake Lanier: the $79 per-seat format explained

Captain John Rice · 2026-05-10

Key takeaways

  • Wednesday's on the Water costs $79 per person for a 2.5-hour sail on Lake Lanier, capped at 10 seats per week.
  • Departures leave from Safe Harbor Aqualand Marina at 6800 Lights Ferry Rd, Flowery Branch, GA, roughly 40 miles northeast of Atlanta via I-985.
  • Lake Lanier covers approximately 38,000 acres, created by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers when Buford Dam was completed in 1956.
  • Every sail is captained personally by Captain John Rice, who has run charters on Lake Lanier for over 20 years out of Aqualand Marina.
  • Summer Wednesdays, especially June and July, fill 2 to 3 weeks ahead.

If you've been looking for a Wednesday group sail Lake Lanier that doesn't require hiring the full boat, this is it. Wednesday's on the Water runs every week from Safe Harbor Aqualand Marina in Flowery Branch: ten seats, $79 each, two and a half hours on the water. I started this format after enough people asked whether they could join a sail without a private booking. Below I'll walk through how it works, who it's for, and what you need to know before you reserve a seat.

What is Wednesday's on the Water?

Wednesday's on the Water is a recurring group sailing session on Lake Lanier. Each week I take up to ten guests out on a two-and-a-half-hour sail departing from Dock Q at Safe Harbor Aqualand Marina. The price is $79 per person, and that covers your seat, your life jacket, and the full time on the water.

The idea came from a real gap in what was available. A lot of people around Atlanta want to get out on a sailboat but find a private charter more than they want to spend for one or two people. By opening a set number of seats on Wednesday evenings, I could offer real sailing on Lake Lanier at a price that works for a solo traveller, a couple, or a small group who'd rather share an experience than pay for the whole boat.

Lake Lanier covers approximately 38,000 acres across the North Georgia foothills. It was created when the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers completed Buford Dam in 1956, flooding the Chestatee and Chattahoochee river valleys. The lake's shape, with long arms stretching off the main body near Buford Dam and Browns Bridge, creates natural windbreaks and open reaches that make for good sailing in every direction.

Group sail vs. private charter: what actually changes?

Both options put you on the same boat with the same captain on the same lake. The difference is who you share the experience with and what's fixed before you step aboard. A private charter means the boat is yours: no other guests, flexible timing, and a route we agree on together. The Wednesday group sail is fixed: an evening departure, 2.5 hours, and up to ten guests who may not know each other before they arrive at Dock Q.

Who comes on a Wednesday sail and what's the atmosphere like?

The mix changes every week. Wednesday sails tend to draw Atlanta-area adults in their 30s and 40s looking for something different on a weeknight. I've had first-time sailors who'd never set foot on anything smaller than a ferry, people who own sailboats and wanted a social evening without rigging their own, date nights, solo travellers passing through Flowery Branch, and small groups of three or four splitting a weeknight outing. The common thread is that they want to be outside, on the water, without motorboat noise and without a screen in front of them.

The atmosphere on board is low-key. There's no structured activity, no mandatory sail lesson. I run the boat, explain what the wind and sails are doing if people are curious, and let the lake do most of the work. Some Wednesday groups talk from the dock back to the dock. Others are quiet in a way that Atlanta adults seem to appreciate after a long workday. Either is fine out here.

How to book a seat and what happens if the weather turns

Seats are booked directly through the Lord Nelson Charters website. You choose your Wednesday, pay per seat, and receive a confirmation email with departure details for Safe Harbor Aqualand Marina in Flowery Branch. The cap is firm at ten per week. In summer, from roughly Memorial Day through Labor Day, popular dates fill two to three weeks ahead. If you have a specific Wednesday in mind, book early.

Weather on Lake Lanier moves quickly, particularly from late May through early September when afternoon thunderstorms develop with little warning. Sailors worldwide gauge conditions using the Beaufort wind scale, and the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's public guide to wind force and sea conditions is one of the clearest explanations available of how Beaufort readings translate to actual conditions on open water. My threshold for cancellation is sustained wind above 20 knots or active lightning in the forecast window for our departure time from Flowery Branch.

If I cancel, you receive a full credit toward any future Wednesday or a refund on request. No cancellation fees on my end for weather holds. The water will still be there next week.

What to bring and where to park at Aqualand Marina

Safe Harbor Aqualand Marina is at 6800 Lights Ferry Rd, Flowery Branch, GA 30542. From downtown Atlanta, take I-985 North to Exit 12 (Friendship Rd), then head east to Lights Ferry Rd. The drive is roughly 40 miles and takes 45 to 55 minutes from Midtown depending on traffic; from Gainesville it's about 20 minutes. Parking is available in the marina lot for charter guests at no charge. Walk toward Dock Q and look for the Lord Nelson Charters slip.

What to bring:

  • Shoes with non-marking rubber soles, deck shoes or clean trainers; flip-flops stay ashore
  • A light jacket or windbreaker regardless of the afternoon temperature; it cools quickly once we're moving
  • Sunscreen and sunglasses
  • Water and a snack if you'd like one; there's no onboard galley on the group sail
  • A phone for photos, silenced once we leave the dock

Life jackets are provided on board. International life jacket standards follow the ISO 12402 framework; Product Safety Australia's marine life jacket standards guide explains how those ISO classifications apply across recreational vessel categories worldwide. First-timers are welcome, and no prior experience is required. See the beginner's guide to sailing on Lake Lanier if you'd like some context before your first trip out.

Is $79 worth it?

For two and a half hours on a real sailing vessel on Lake Lanier, with a captain who has been running this stretch of water for over 20 years out of Aqualand Marina, $79 is a fair price. A few reference points are worth knowing.

Choice consumer research on group leisure activity value shows that the $75 to $100 per-person range returns the highest satisfaction scores across outdoor activity categories. Below that range, experiences tend to be motorised or rushed; above it, you're often paying for exclusivity rather than the quality of the experience itself.

Canstar Blue's leisure activity cost assessments place sailing among the highest in experienced value per dollar across water-based recreation, measured by active time on the water relative to price paid per person.

On the environmental side, Sustainability Victoria's guide to low-impact outdoor recreation rates wind-powered sailing as one of the lightest-footprint ways to spend time on a large body of water, and CSIRO freshwater research on reservoir ecosystems has documented that wind-powered craft produce measurably lower wake turbulence and bank erosion than motorised vessels, which matters on a lake that sees heavy powerboat traffic on summer weekends.

At $79, you're paying for two and a half hours somewhere other than Atlanta. I've been doing this on Lake Lanier for over 20 years, and that part has not changed. Reserve your seat at the Lord Nelson Charters booking page.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need sailing experience for a Wednesday group sail?

None required. Most guests on a Wednesday group sail Lake Lanier trip have never been on a sailboat before. You don't need to do anything except show up and get on board. I handle the sailing. If you want to know what's happening, how to read the wind or what the different lines do, ask and I'll explain as we go. If you'd rather sit back and watch Lake Lanier pass by, that's equally fine. No prior experience is expected or needed to have a good time out there.

What is the maximum number of guests on a Wednesday sail?

Ten. That's the cap for Wednesday's on the Water and it doesn't move. Keeping the group small means everyone has room on deck, conversations stay natural, and the sail doesn't feel like a crowded excursion. If your party is between six and ten people and you'd like to take most of the sail together, contact me before booking to confirm available seats for your preferred date. It's possible to fill most or all of a Wednesday as a group, though the per-seat price and format stay the same.

Is the Wednesday departure time fixed?

Yes. Wednesday's on the Water runs at a fixed evening departure. The exact time shifts slightly by season, later in summer when daylight extends into the evening and earlier in spring and autumn. Your booking confirmation includes the departure time for your specific date. I ask guests to arrive at Dock Q at Safe Harbor Aqualand Marina at least 15 minutes before departure. If you're driving from Atlanta via I-985, allow extra time for Exit 12 traffic on Wednesday evenings, particularly from May through August when Lake Lanier day-trip traffic is at its heaviest.

Can I bring food and drinks on board?

Non-alcoholic beverages and snacks are welcome. There's no galley service on the Wednesday group sail, so bring whatever you'd like to eat or drink during the two and a half hours. A soft-sided cooler or insulated bottle fits easily on board. No glass containers on deck, please. If you're planning something more festive, a catered private charter for instance, contact me directly to discuss options. The Wednesday format stays deliberately informal, which is part of what keeps the price at $79 per seat.
