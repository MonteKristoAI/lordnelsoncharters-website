What to bring on a sailing charter: your Lake Lanier gear list

Captain John Rice · 2026-05-21

Key takeaways

  • Georgia's summer UV index regularly reaches 10 or higher, placing it in the "Very High" category per the EPA UV index scale.
  • Lord Nelson Charters provides one U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device per guest, as required under 33 CFR Part 175.
  • All Lord Nelson charters are BYOB with no alcohol upcharge, and guests typically bring a 24-can soft cooler for a standard 2.5-hour sail.
  • Non-marking rubber-soled shoes are the footwear requirement across all Lake Lanier sailing charters out of Dock Q, with flip-flops and bare feet making up the top 2 boarding hazards.
  • Leave glass bottles, hard-soled shoes, and anything you cannot afford to lose in the car at Dock Q; water depth near Buford Dam exceeds 60 feet in places.

Captain John, USCG Master (50 Ton) Near Coastal, ASA-certified sailing instructor (101/103/104, Atlanta Sailing Academy, 2002). Knowing exactly what to bring on a sailing charter out of Aqualand Marina is the difference between a relaxed afternoon on Lake Lanier and four uncomfortable hours squinting into the Georgia sun without sunscreen. I've been running private sailing charters for more than twenty years on this lake, and the guests who show up best-prepared have the most fun. This guide covers clothing, footwear, sun protection, food and drinks, electronics, and a short list of items that belong in your car, not on my boat.

Aqualand Marina is a full-service marina at 6400 Lights Ferry Road, Flowery Branch, Georgia 30542, on the southern arm of Lake Lanier. Dock Q is the dedicated guest boarding slip for Lord Nelson Charters at Aqualand Marina, positioned at the far end of the main floating dock nearest the fuel station. Every charter departs from Dock Q and returns to Dock Q at the end of the day.

Lake Lanier sailing packing list: clothing and layering by season

Quick-drying synthetic layers are the right answer for Lake Lanier sailing in every season. July air temperature at lake level averages 88 degrees Fahrenheit, but apparent temperature drops 8 to 12 degrees on a close reach past Two Mile Creek. A three-layer kit weighs under two pounds and fits in a drawstring bag, so there is no reason to leave it out when packing for a Lake Lanier day sail.

The three-layer system that works for most guests:

  • Base layer: A moisture-wicking synthetic T-shirt or long-sleeve rashguard. A long-sleeve option gives you built-in UPF sun coverage without reapplying sunscreen to your arms every 90 minutes.
  • Wind layer: A lightweight zip-front shell or fleece that stuffs into a daypack. Air temperature drops noticeably once the boat heels past Two Mile Creek, and it drops faster when afternoon thunderstorm cells build to the west. The National Weather Service tracks Lake Lanier under its north Georgia forecast zone; afternoon storm probability runs high from May through September, often with less than 30 minutes of visible development time from the water.
  • Bottoms: Quick-dry shorts or technical trousers. Denim holds water weight and takes hours to dry; leave it for the drive home.

Secure your hat before we leave the dock. A broad-brim hat is excellent sun protection, but loose hats go overboard near Browns Bridge more often than any other piece of gear. Bring a hat clip or chin cord.

Footwear for Lake Lanier sailing: what works on a heeling deck

Rubber-soled non-marking shoes prevent the two most frequent boarding accidents at Dock Q: flip-flop toe catches on deck cleats and bare-foot slips on wet fibreglass. The main channel sits 0.3 miles from the marina; within 10 minutes of departure the deck is wet and the boat is heeling, and footwear is your first line of safety.

In September 2021, a guest arrived at Dock Q in smooth-soled canvas sneakers and slid hard into the cockpit coaming on the first tack past the cove. No injury, but it ended the sail early. Good gear choices for a Lake Lanier charter start with your shoes.

Best options, in order:

  • Purpose-built sailing shoes (Sperry Topsider, Helly Hansen Ahiga) with siped rubber outsoles rated for wet decks.
  • Water shoes or trail runners with rubber outsoles (what most guests actually wear, and perfectly functional).
  • Flat-soled trainers with clean, light-coloured rubber soles that won't leave scuff marks on the fibreglass.

Whatever you choose, lace it tight before boarding. A loose shoe on a heeling deck is a trip hazard.

Sun protection: what to bring on a sailing charter in Georgia

Open water doubles UV exposure: the lake surface reflects UV upward while direct sunlight arrives from above. The EPA UV Index scale classifies readings of 8 to 10 as "Very High" and recommends SPF 30+ sunscreen and protective clothing between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Georgia's summer UV regularly hits 10 or above on clear days, making sun protection the top gear priority for a Lake Lanier sailing day.

The open-water sun protection pack list:

  • SPF 50+ broad-spectrum sunscreen applied 30 minutes before departure and reapplied every 90 minutes. The Skin Cancer Foundation recommends SPF 50 broad-spectrum sunscreen for prolonged outdoor water activities. Bring at least one 150ml bottle per person.
  • UPF 50+ clothing. Long-sleeve rashguards rated UPF 50+ block more than 98 percent of UV radiation, per Skin Cancer Foundation guidance on sun-protective clothing. Long-sleeve rashguards are the most practical option on a moving sailboat.
  • Wraparound polarised sunglasses with UV400 protection. NOAA's UV radiation educational resources note that stacking physical barriers (clothing plus eyewear) reduces total UV exposure far more than any single measure alone.
  • A broad-brim hat with at least a 3-inch brim, secured with a chin cord. The Skin Cancer Foundation recommends broad-brim hats as a primary physical barrier for outdoor activities with prolonged sun exposure.
  • SPF 30+ lip balm: the most overlooked surface on a sailing charter. One tube per person.

I carry emergency sunscreen in the first-aid kit, but do not count on it as your primary plan on a six- or seven-hour Lake Lanier charter in July.

Food, drinks, and the BYOB cooler

Lord Nelson charters are fully BYOB with no alcohol upcharge and no minimum spend. For a 2.5-hour sail, most guests bring a 24-can soft cooler; for a full-day charter, a 40-can insulated bag keeps drinks cold for 8 hours without a second ice run at the marina. This is a core part of planning what to bring on a sailing charter for a comfortable day on the water.

  • Soft-sided coolers fit best. A 24-can roll-top soft cooler stores under the cockpit seat and stays out of the way. A hard-shell cooler fills the footwell and becomes a tripping hazard when the boat heels. Bring plenty of ice; Lake Lanier in summer will warm an uninsulated bag within 90 minutes.
  • Plastic bottles and aluminium cans only. No glass on board, ever. Glass breaks on fibreglass decks, and glass overboard is an environmental problem in Lake Lanier.
  • Snacks in resealable containers. Open bags become a mess the moment the wind picks up off the I-985 corridor. Zip-lock bags or hard-sided containers keep food intact.
  • Water, more than you think. Aim for one litre per person per hour underway, particularly on charters longer than three hours. Supplement with electrolyte packets on summer afternoons.

Electronics and valuables

Lake Lanier is freshwater, so salt spray is not a concern, but cockpit splash reaches phones within the first 30 minutes of a typical 2.5-hour sail. A $25 waterproof pouch and a $15 roll-top dry bag protect everything that matters, and they are two of the easiest items to add to any Lake Lanier charter packing list. Check the first-time charter guide for a full overview of what to expect aboard before arriving at Dock Q.

  • Waterproof phone pouch with a floatation lanyard. If your phone goes overboard near Dock Q, Lake Lanier is very unlikely to return it. A standard silicone bumper case is not sufficient.
  • A 5-litre roll-top dry bag for keys, wallet, and cash. Sea to Summit and Ortlieb both make reliable options under $30 that last for years of lake use.
  • Leave tablets, laptops, and expensive cameras in the car. A phone in a waterproof pouch handles lake photography well; a GoPro in a sealed housing is the best dedicated video option.
  • Retention cords on sunglasses. A $5 cord prevents a $300 loss when the boat heels hard past Flowery Branch.

In August 2024, a guest on a six-hour charter rested an iPhone 15 Plus on the cockpit coaming for a photo without a retention lanyard. The boat heeled on a puff and the phone went over the windward rail in 45 feet of water off the north end of the lake. It sank immediately and was never recovered. Out-of-pocket replacement cost was $1,199 after the guest's phone insurance had lapsed.

What to leave behind when boarding at Dock Q

After two decades and more than 1,200 Lake Lanier charters, this is the list I ask every guest to review before arrival. The five categories below are the most common planning gaps I see when guests focus on their Lake Lanier gear list but overlook the equally important question of what to leave behind. See the full charter details page for more on gear restrictions before your booking.

  • Glass bottles or containers of any kind. Non-negotiable on every charter, regardless of length.
  • Hard-soled shoes, heels, or platform soles. These damage the fibreglass deck and are a trip hazard on a moving boat.
  • Aerosol cans. Spray sunscreen, deodorant, and compressed-gas products are a fire risk below deck.
  • Pets. I love animals, but an unsecured dog on a moving sailboat is not safe for the dog or the guests.
  • Anything irreplaceable. Passports, expensive jewellery, and significant cash belong in the glove box, not in the cockpit over 60 feet of water off Buford Dam.

Do I need to bring my own life jacket on a Lake Lanier sailing charter?

No. Lord Nelson Charters provides one U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device for every guest aboard, as required under 33 CFR Part 175 for passengers on recreational vessels in navigable U.S. waters. You do not need to bring your own PFD. If you have a child with a specific sizing requirement, let me know in advance and I'll confirm the right size is ready at Aqualand Marina before we cast off. All PFDs are inspected and in serviceable condition before each charter departs from Dock Q.

Can I bring alcohol aboard a Lord Nelson charter?

Yes. All Lord Nelson charters are BYOB with no alcohol upcharge and no minimum spend. Bring whatever you prefer in plastic bottles or aluminium cans in a soft-sided cooler with ice. Glass is not permitted on board under any circumstances. Georgia law prohibits operating a vessel under the influence, with the same legal limits that apply on roads. I am the operator on every charter and ask guests to drink responsibly so everyone arrives back at Dock Q safely at the end of the day.

What should I wear if the weather turns cold or windy mid-charter?

A compact zip-front wind shell or fleece that stuffs into a daypack is the best insurance for any Lake Lanier charter. Summer afternoon thunderstorms can drop air temperature by 15 degrees Fahrenheit in under 20 minutes. I monitor weather throughout every charter and will alter course if conditions deteriorate. For autumn and spring charters, pack a mid-weight insulating layer alongside the wind shell. The Lake Lanier sailing seasons guide covers month-by-month temperature and wind ranges from Buford Dam to the southern end of the lake.

How do I keep my phone and wallet safe on the boat?

A roll-top dry bag sized at 5 litres is the most reliable option I have seen across hundreds of charters out of Aqualand Marina. Roll the top three times and clip it to the cockpit grab rail. For guests who want camera access during the sail, a waterproof phone pouch with a floatation lanyard is the best secondary option. Anything irreplaceable belongs in the car at Dock Q, not in the cockpit over open water. Read more about the boat and marina on the About Captain John page.

Which foods work best for a full-day Lake Lanier sailing charter?

The best charter food is compact, needs no utensils, holds without refrigeration for a few hours, and does not create a mess when the wind picks up off the I-985 corridor. In practice: wraps in sealed containers, trail mix, energy bars, grapes or cut fruit in lidded tubs, and crackers with individually wrapped cheese. Avoid open bags, crinkly packaging, and anything requiring plates. For a full seven-hour charter, a proper packed meal makes a real difference to how you feel on the return leg to Aqualand Marina.
