Knowing exactly what to bring on a sailing charter out of Aqualand Marina is the difference between a relaxed afternoon on Lake Lanier and four uncomfortable hours squinting into the Georgia sun without sunscreen. I've been running private sailing charters for more than twenty years on this lake, and the guests who show up best-prepared have the most fun. This guide covers clothing, footwear, sun protection, food and drinks, electronics, and a short list of items that belong in your car, not on my boat.

Aqualand Marina is a full-service marina at 6400 Lights Ferry Road, Flowery Branch, Georgia 30542, on the southern arm of Lake Lanier, a 38,000-acre reservoir in Hall County, Georgia. Dock Q is the dedicated guest boarding slip for Lord Nelson Charters at Aqualand Marina, positioned at the far end of the main floating dock nearest the fuel station. Every charter departs from Dock Q and returns to Dock Q at the end of the day.

The gear list for a Lake Lanier charter is shorter than most guests expect, but each item on it is there for a specific reason. The lake's UV index reaches 10 or higher between late May and early September. Water depth near Buford Dam exceeds 60 feet. Afternoon storm cells off the I-985 corridor can build to a charter-ending intensity in under 30 minutes from the first visible signs. Pack for those three conditions and the rest of the charter handles itself.

Lake Lanier sailing packing list: clothing and layering by season

Quick-drying synthetic layers are the correct answer for Lake Lanier sailing in all four seasons. July lake-level air temperature averages 88°F, but apparent temperature drops 8 to 12 degrees on a close reach past Two Mile Creek, and afternoon storm cells west of I-985 can cut temperature further in under 20 minutes.

When deciding what to bring on a sailing charter for clothing, the three-layer system for a Lake Lanier departure consists of a moisture-wicking synthetic base layer, a wind-blocking mid-layer shell, and quick-dry bottoms, following the same packing framework taught at ASA-certified sailing schools operating on Georgia lakes. A long-sleeve rashguard rated UPF 50+ doubles as the base layer and provides more than 98 percent UV blockade without requiring reapplication, a significant advantage over sunscreen alone during a six- or seven-hour open-water charter where SPF 50 must be reapplied every 90 minutes. The wind shell or lightweight fleece stuffs into a daypack weighing under 300 grams; its value is highest from May through September, when afternoon convective storm cells build west of the I-985 corridor and can drive a 15-degree Fahrenheit temperature drop in under 20 minutes on the open water between Two Mile Creek and Browns Bridge. Quick-dry technical shorts or trousers complete the kit; denim is the worst fabric choice for sailing because it holds water weight, reduces mobility on a heeling deck, and takes more than four hours to dry in summer humidity at Lake Lanier. The full three-layer kit weighs under two pounds and packs into a drawstring bag, making it the easiest and most impactful clothing decision on any Lake Lanier charter gear list.

The three-layer system that works for most guests:

  • Base layer: A moisture-wicking synthetic T-shirt or long-sleeve rashguard. A long-sleeve option gives you built-in UPF sun coverage without reapplying sunscreen to your arms every 90 minutes.
  • Wind layer: A lightweight zip-front shell or fleece that stuffs into a daypack. Air temperature drops noticeably once the boat heels past Two Mile Creek, and it drops faster when afternoon thunderstorm cells build to the west. The National Weather Service tracks Lake Lanier under its north Georgia forecast zone; afternoon storm probability runs high from May through September, often with less than 30 minutes of visible development time from the water.
  • Bottoms: Quick-dry shorts or technical trousers. Denim holds water weight and takes hours to dry; leave it for the drive home.

Secure your hat before we leave the dock. A broad-brim hat is excellent sun protection, but loose hats go overboard near Browns Bridge more often than any other piece of gear. Bring a hat clip or chin cord.

Footwear on Lake Lanier: what to bring on a sailing charter for deck safety

Footwear tops the list of what to bring on a sailing charter from Dock Q for one safety reason: rubber-soled non-marking shoes prevent the two most common boarding accidents at Aqualand Marina, flip-flop toe catches on deck cleats and bare-foot slips on wet fibreglass. The main channel is 0.3 miles from Aqualand Marina; within 10 minutes of departure the deck is wet and the boat is heeling.

In September 2021, a guest arrived at Dock Q in smooth-soled canvas sneakers and slid hard into the cockpit coaming on the first tack past the cove. No injury, but it ended the sail early. Good gear choices for a Lake Lanier charter start with your shoes.

Best options, in order:

  • Purpose-built sailing shoes (Sperry Topsider, Helly Hansen Ahiga) with siped rubber outsoles rated for wet decks.
  • Water shoes or trail runners with rubber outsoles (what most guests actually wear, and perfectly functional).
  • Flat-soled trainers with clean, light-coloured rubber soles that won't leave scuff marks on the fibreglass.

Whatever you choose, lace it tight before boarding. A loose shoe on a heeling deck is a trip hazard.

Sun protection: what to bring on a sailing charter in Georgia

Lake Lanier sits at approximately 34 degrees north latitude, where the EPA UV Index reaches 10 or 11 from late May through early September, classified "Very High," requiring SPF 30+ sunscreen and protective clothing between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Open water doubles effective UV exposure because the lake surface reflects UV upward while direct sunlight strikes from above.

A 2.5-hour afternoon Lake Lanier charter beginning at 1 p.m. places guests on open water through the full peak UV exposure window of 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. On a full-day seven-hour charter, total UV dose approaches the equivalent of two full beach days at the same latitude. A single 150 ml bottle of SPF 50 sunscreen runs out before the midpoint of a full-day sail when reapplied correctly every 90 minutes across four guests, as recommended by the Skin Cancer Foundation. Long-sleeve rashguards rated UPF 50+ block more than 98 percent of UV radiation without requiring any reapplication, making them the most reliable single piece of sun protection for extended open-water sailing on Lake Lanier. Wraparound UV400 polarized sunglasses filter all UV light at wavelengths up to 400 nanometres and cut glare off the lake surface, a compounding exposure factor that increases total UV dose on the water compared to dry land. SPF 30+ lip balm, one tube per person, is the single most overlooked sun protection item on any Lake Lanier charter packing list.

The sun protection answer to what to bring on a sailing charter on Lake Lanier:

  • SPF 50+ broad-spectrum sunscreen applied 30 minutes before departure and reapplied every 90 minutes. The Skin Cancer Foundation recommends SPF 50 broad-spectrum sunscreen for prolonged outdoor water activities. Bring at least one 150ml bottle per person.
  • UPF 50+ clothing. Long-sleeve rashguards rated UPF 50+ block more than 98 percent of UV radiation, per Skin Cancer Foundation guidance on sun-protective clothing. Long-sleeve rashguards are the most practical option on a moving sailboat.
  • Wraparound polarised sunglasses with UV400 protection (UV400 lenses filter all ultraviolet light at wavelengths up to 400 nanometres). EPA's UV health effects resources note that stacking physical barriers (clothing plus eyewear) reduces total UV exposure far more than any single measure alone.
  • A broad-brim hat with at least a 3-inch brim, secured with a chin cord. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a hat with at least a 3-inch brim as a primary physical barrier for outdoor activities with prolonged sun exposure.
  • SPF 30+ lip balm: the most overlooked surface on a sailing charter. One tube per person.

I carry emergency sunscreen in the first-aid kit, but do not count on it as your primary plan on a six- or seven-hour Lake Lanier charter in July.

Food, drinks, and cooler planning: what to bring on a sailing charter

Lord Nelson Charters runs fully BYOB with no alcohol upcharge and no minimum spend. For a standard 2.5-hour sail, most guests bring a 24-can soft cooler; for a full-day charter, a 40-can insulated bag keeps drinks cold for 8 hours without a second marina run.

  • Soft-sided coolers fit best. A 24-can roll-top soft cooler stores under the cockpit seat and stays out of the way. A hard-shell cooler fills the footwell and becomes a tripping hazard when the boat heels. Bring plenty of ice; Lake Lanier in summer will warm an uninsulated bag within 90 minutes.
  • Plastic bottles and aluminium cans only. No glass on board, ever. Glass breaks on fibreglass decks, and glass overboard is an environmental problem in Lake Lanier.
  • Snacks in resealable containers. Open bags become a mess the moment the wind picks up off the I-985 corridor. Zip-lock bags or hard-sided containers keep food intact.
  • Water, more than you think. Aim for one litre per person per hour underway, particularly on charters longer than three hours. Supplement with electrolyte packets on summer afternoons.

Electronics and valuables

Phones, cameras, and valuables follow the same rule on a Lake Lanier charter: waterproof first, then secure. The lake is freshwater, so salt corrosion is not a factor, but cockpit splash reaches phones within 30 minutes of departure. A $25 waterproof pouch and a $15 dry bag weigh under 100 grams combined.

  • Waterproof phone pouch with a floatation lanyard. If your phone goes overboard near Dock Q, Lake Lanier is very unlikely to return it. A standard silicone bumper case is not sufficient.
  • A 5-litre roll-top dry bag for keys, wallet, and cash. Sea to Summit and Ortlieb both make reliable options under $30 that last for years of lake use.
  • Leave tablets, laptops, and expensive cameras in the car. A phone in a waterproof pouch handles lake photography well; a GoPro in a sealed housing is the best dedicated video option.
  • Retention cords on sunglasses. A $5 cord prevents a $300 loss when the boat heels hard past Flowery Branch.

The boat heeled on a puff and the phone went over the windward rail in 45 feet of water off the north end of the lake. It sank immediately and was never recovered. Out-of-pocket replacement cost was $1,199 after the guest's phone insurance had lapsed.

What to leave behind when boarding at Dock Q

Knowing what to bring on a sailing charter from Dock Q requires equal attention to the leave-behind list. Glass bottles, hard-soled shoes, and aerosol cans are the three categories turned away most often at Dock Q, Aqualand Marina. Across more than 1,200 Lake Lanier charter departures since 2002, those three categories account for nearly all boarding delays and on-deck incidents logged by Lord Nelson Charters.   • Glass bottles or containers of any kind. Non-negotiable on every charter, regardless of length.
  • Hard-soled shoes, heels, or platform soles. These damage the fibreglass deck and are a trip hazard on a moving boat.
  • Aerosol cans. Spray sunscreen, deodorant, and compressed-gas products are a fire risk below deck.
  • Pets. I love animals, but an unsecured dog on a moving sailboat is not safe for the dog or the guests.
  • Anything irreplaceable. Passports, expensive jewellery, and significant cash belong in the glove box, not in the cockpit over 60 feet of water off Buford Dam.

Do I need to bring my own life jacket on a Lake Lanier sailing charter?

No. Guests on a Lake Lanier sailing charter with Lord Nelson Charters do not need to bring their own life jacket. Lord Nelson Charters provides one U.S. Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device (PFD) for every guest, as required under 33 CFR Part 175 for passengers on recreational vessels in navigable U.S. waters. All PFDs are inspected before each departure from Dock Q at Aqualand Marina, 6400 Lights Ferry Road, Flowery Branch, Georgia 30542. Parents of children who need a specific PFD size should notify Captain John Rice in advance so the correct size is staged and confirmed before the charter casts off.

Can I bring alcohol aboard a Lord Nelson charter?

Yes. All Lake Lanier sailing charters with Lord Nelson Charters are fully BYOB with no alcohol upcharge and no minimum spend. Guests may bring beer, wine, or spirits in plastic bottles or aluminium cans inside a soft-sided cooler with ice. Glass containers are not permitted on board under any circumstances. Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 52-7-12) prohibits operating a vessel under the influence, applying the same 0.08 BAC limit as road vehicles. Captain John Rice personally operates every charter and asks guests to drink responsibly so everyone returns safely to Dock Q, Aqualand Marina, at the end of the day.

What should I wear if the weather turns cold or windy mid-charter?

Pack a compact zip-front wind shell or fleece that stuffs into a daypack on any Lake Lanier sailing charter, regardless of season. Summer afternoon thunderstorms on Lake Lanier can drop air temperature by 15 degrees Fahrenheit in under 20 minutes, particularly when storm cells build west of the I-985 corridor. Captain John Rice monitors NOAA weather throughout every charter and alters course if conditions deteriorate. For autumn and spring charters departing from Aqualand Marina, add a mid-weight insulating layer: October lake-level air temperature averages 62°F but can fall below 50°F on a northwest wind, making layering the only reliable strategy from September onward.

How do I keep my phone and wallet safe on the boat?

A 5-litre roll-top dry bag is the most reliable method for protecting phones and wallets on a Lake Lanier sailing charter: roll the top three times and clip it to the cockpit grab rail. Pair it with a waterproof phone pouch on a floatation lanyard for camera access during the sail. In August 2024, a guest on a Lord Nelson Charters six-hour sail lost an iPhone 15 Plus overboard in 45 feet of water near the north end of Lake Lanier; it was never recovered and the out-of-pocket replacement cost was $1,199. Anything irreplaceable belongs in the car at Dock Q, Aqualand Marina.

Which foods work best for a full-day Lake Lanier sailing charter?

For a full-day Lake Lanier sailing charter, pack compact food that needs no utensils and stays intact when wind picks up: wraps in sealed containers, trail mix, energy bars, cut fruit in lidded tubs, and crackers with individually wrapped cheese. Avoid open bags, crinkly packaging, and anything requiring plates; wind off the I-985 corridor turns loose food into deck debris within seconds. Aim for one litre of water per person per hour underway; a seven-hour summer charter means at least seven litres per person. Electrolyte packets supplement water effectively on full-day summer charters departing from Aqualand Marina, Flowery Branch.
